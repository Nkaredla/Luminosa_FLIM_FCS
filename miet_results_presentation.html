The measurement

Lifetime becomes height

A gold film quenches a nearby fluorophore at a rate that depends steeply on its distance from the metal, over roughly the first 100 nm. Reading the membrane dye's fluorescence lifetime pixel by pixel and inverting that dependence turns a lifetime image into a height image with nanometre precision in z.

Two dyes are recorded in one pass by pulsed interleaved excitation: MemGlow on the cell membrane in the 640 nm window, and CD58–Atto488 on the bilayer beneath in the 485 nm window. The two images are therefore perfectly registered — same scan, same pixels, no alignment step.

Layer thicknesses are labelled, not to scale. The quantum yield and free-space lifetime are anchored on the bare bilayer, which sits about 4 nm above the silica and is uniformly labelled, so it provides a known height to calibrate against.

Findings

Three results, at three levels of confidence

The label on each block says how far to trust it. Only the third is a clean negative; the first is exploratory, and the second is limited by how many pixels invert.

Finding 1 Exploratory

Cells prepared at room temperature are flattened in the middle with a raised rim. Cold-prepared cells are the other way round.

Splitting each footprint into a core (beyond 55 % of the way in from the edge) and a rim (the outer 20 %) and differencing the median heights separates the two preparations almost perfectly. All six room-temperature cells have a core lower than their rim, by 12 to 35 nm. Nine of eleven cold-prepared cells go the other way, by up to 52 nm.

A central flattening with a raised skirt is the geometry expected of a spreading adhesion zone, and the room-temperature cells also spread over about 65 % more area. But two cautions apply, and they are the reason this is labelled exploratory rather than established.

Why this is exploratory

The core-minus-rim metric was chosen after looking at the height maps, so its p‑value is not the p‑value of a pre-specified test. And the two groups are not measured equally well: 64–90 % of pixels invert at room temperature against only 8–55 % when cold-prepared, so the cold rim estimates rest on far fewer pixels. Restricting to the six cold cells above 30 % inversion keeps the direction but weakens it to p = 0.030.

Finding 2 Robust

Membrane closer than 30 nm is rare. Thirteen of seventeen cells have none at all.

Applying one fixed threshold to every cell — median-filtered height below 30 nm, in a connected region larger than a diffraction spot — yields contact regions in only four cells, three of them cold-prepared. Three have unusually low median heights, at 19, 28 and 56 nm against about 74 nm across the set; the fourth contributes a single region covering 0.4 % of its footprint.

That thirteen cells return nothing is a result, not a gap. A per-cell adaptive threshold, such as the lower mode of the height histogram, would have produced regions in all seventeen and turned this absence into a dataset. Contact is a physical claim, so the cut stays a number in nanometres and is printed on every figure.

Finding 3 Negative, and one direction is confounded

No cell shows CD58 enriched at its contacts. Every measurable effect points the other way — which is also what an optical artefact would produce.

For each cell the CD58 signal inside the contact regions is compared with the footprint mean, and read against a null built by rotating each region about the footprint centroid through a random admissible angle. Rotation is the right randomisation here because contacts sit near the rim, where CD58 is systematically lower; a null that moved regions inward would compare rim pixels with middle pixels and report that shared geometry as association.

Enrichment appears in none of the four cells. One of twelve CD58 clusters falls inside a contact region, against about three expected by area. The only nominally significant result, on cell 133830, is a depletion at p = 0.007.

Why the depletion cannot be interpreted

The cell body sits above the bilayer and attenuates both the 485 nm excitation and the Atto488 emission. Wherever the membrane is closer there is more material in the light path, so CD58 counts fall for purely optical reasons. That artefact produces depletion and only depletion — which is why the absence of enrichment is trustworthy while the depletion is not.

A competing biological reading survives equally well: the contact regions invert to 12–14 nm, shorter than the CD58 ectodomain at roughly 13 nm, so size-based exclusion of the kind kinetic-segregation models predict would look exactly like this. These data cannot separate the two.

What is not settled

Two of these bear directly on the absolute height scale, so they propagate into every number above.

  • Which lifetime is the bilayerThe bilayer decay is biexponential, with components near 0.33 and 0.91 ns. Whether the short one is an instrument-response tail or genuine bilayer emission changes the anchored quantum yield and shifts every height. Both variants are on disk; this presentation uses the second. A bare chip with spacer, no bilayer and no dye would decide it in one measurement.
  • The shadowing confoundCell-body attenuation of the 485 nm path mimics CD58 depletion at contacts. It cannot produce enrichment, so it does not threaten Finding 3's negative — but it blocks any positive interpretation of the depletion.
  • Sparse inversion when cold-prepared8–55 % of pixels invert against 64–90 % at room temperature. Interpolated pixels are marked and excluded from every statistic, but they limit how finely the cold maps can be read.
  • Rim contaminationEvery contact region found sits within a few pixels of the footprint edge, where membrane and bare-bilayer signal mix and bias the lifetime low. Some of the 12–14 nm may be that mixing rather than true approach. Per-region edge distance is recorded and plotted so it stays visible.
  • Interior contactsAt 30 nm the detector finds only the lamellipodial rim. Raising the threshold to 50 or 60 nm would reach the broad central zones the room-temperature cells clearly have, at the cost of “contact” meaning something looser.
